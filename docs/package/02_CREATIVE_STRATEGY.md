# Creative Strategy — Touchform.md

## Creative thesis
Do not lead with “NFC stands.” Lead with what becomes possible when a physical object opens a digital experience. Make the customer discover the problem/idea/solution, then let them see it take physical form.

## Signature experience 1: The Business Solution Consultant
A conversational workspace, not a generic support widget. The visitor starts with a natural prompt: “Tell me about your business and one thing you wish customers did more easily.” The AI asks targeted follow-ups, proactively suggests ideas, compares them, and assembles a concise custom offer. Provide sample starter prompts to prevent a blank-chat problem. At the end, show three connected pieces: recommended physical object, phone-side experience, and why it addresses the stated friction.

## Signature experience 2: Physical + Digital Builder
Split view: the object preview on one side and simulated smartphone experience on the other. Changing color, material, shape, text or destination updates the relevant views. The visual generator receives the canonical JSON specification; it does not invent the selected product's facts or price. Standard products use deterministic layers/templates wherever fidelity matters.

## Signature experience 3: Tap Demo
Click/tap the physical stand and watch the phone UI change to demonstrate menu, contact, review, booking, call staff, or another configured action. Clearly label the interaction as a site demo, not a live NFC read.

## Hero art direction: “One object. Many worlds.”
A cinematic product shot with one identical stand anchored in the same central position and scale while the setting changes around it: cafe counter → modern reception → office/event → boutique/hotel. The object silhouette, material, branding and camera angle should not morph between cuts. Use short, elegant crossfades/match cuts and restrained soundless motion. Pair with a crisp static frame for reduced-motion and poor-network fallbacks. Do not block content or primary CTA while video loads. If a consistent AI video cannot be achieved, build the sequence from matched stills/foreground compositing or use real footage; do not accept noticeable product drift.

## Homepage sequence
1. **Hero:** short cinematic transformation; headline connects physical and digital; clear routes “Explore ready products” / “Find a solution for my business.”
2. **Talk to the consultant:** embedded preview or obvious launcher, example questions and visible sample output.
3. **Tap Demo:** visitor directly experiences an example phone-side flow.
4. **Choose a route:** standard stands, explore ideas/outcomes, create a custom product.
5. **Product catalog:** product categories organized by both object and desired outcome; standard unit and volume pricing visible.
6. **Cases:** Cucurigo, Délice d’Ange, Padel Point with real product, phone demo, what was tailored.
7. **How it works:** discuss → concept → confirm quote/proof → produce/configure. Keep claims on timing and fulfillment unpublished until verified.
8. **Trust and FAQ:** NFC vs QR, what opens after tap, color/design options, production approval, privacy, how AI visuals work.
9. **Final conversion:** “Have an idea? Describe it.” Plus standard product CTA.

## Conversion system
- Keep one primary action per section and clear secondary action; no competing CTA clutter.
- CTAs are contextual: “Build this stand,” “Get ideas for my business,” “Generate a concept,” “Send concept to manager,” “Order this configuration.”
- Show a compact summary before handoff so the customer knows exactly what the manager will receive.
- When a user has used both image generations, change the action state to “Refine this with our manager”; do not allow hidden repeat calls.
- Bot handoff status is real and explicit: sending / sent / failed. If failed, show fallback options.
- In the concept result, put the personalized offer beside the generated image; AI must sell the solution, not just the artwork.

## Motion principles
- Hybrid of cinematic signature motion and high-speed UI. Motion should explain a causal change (tap opens phone flow, color changes the same object, background transitions while object remains identical).
- Use restrained scroll choreography, subtle state transitions, and one strong hero moment. No scroll-jacking, perpetual movement, cursor gimmicks, giant slow animations or autoplay audio.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; static/low-motion fallback; lazy-load large media; no motion dependency for comprehension or conversion.

## Visual identity / logo brief
Create at least three distinct identity directions, not minor variations:
1. **The threshold:** an abstract frame/portal where physical becomes digital.
2. **The continuous touchpoint:** a custom geometric connection/bridge motif.
3. **The typographic signature:** distinctive Touchform wordmark with one ownable altered letterform or ligature.
The standalone mark must work at favicon scale, monochrome, on colored/wood/acrylic products and in single-color printing/engraving. Use wordmark + symbol + monochrome lockup + favicon/app icon. Avoid a generic NFC/radio-wave icon as the main logo and avoid mimicking Google. Research actual reference brands and logo usage; do not merely render a polished text prompt and call that brand design.

Brand-name handling: use `Touchform.md` as instructed by the founder; do not silently rename. Before public launch, verify ownership/trademark/domain collision because the name appeared in earlier discussion as a local brand reference. If a conflict appears, report evidence and pause.

## Creative test plan
Before committing, compare three distinct art directions using the same content and product assets. Score: clarity, perceived quality, ownability, product credibility, conversion path, mobile usability, performance. Use real-browser screenshots at mobile/tablet/desktop and show the founder visual evidence.
